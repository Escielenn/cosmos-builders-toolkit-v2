// CHAPTER VII · SCENE 03 · DRAFT 2

The negotiation at low tide

SECOND DRAFT POV · IREN CALDIS FLATS · LOW TIDE · 2,184 W · ~9 MIN
· · ·

The tide had gone out far enough that the flats looked, from where Iren stood, like a slate the sea had only half-erased — a long, glistening table of mud and bone-pale stones running flat to the horizon, broken only by the black line of the salt meridian and, at its center, the priest.

She did not, even now, allow herself the priest's name. She had practiced this on the walk down. The priest, she said inside her chest. The man who came for the boy. Names were a kind of grammar, and there were grammars she could not afford to give him today.

He waited with his hands folded in front of him as if waiting were a posture he had been born into. The wind off the sea moved his robe but not his hands. Behind him, very faintly, she could hear the surf, three minutes' walk away, breathing.

She came down the last of the tide-ladders and stepped onto the wet sand and felt, immediately, the shock of cold through the soles of her boots. She had forgotten she was going to be cold. She had been, for two days, only afraid.

"You came," he said.

"I came."

He let the silence work for a moment. She let it work back. The salt meridian — that hand-wide stripe of pale crust the sea drew once a year and never twice in the same place — lay between them, neither his side nor hers, and they had both been raised with strong feelings about which side of it a person should stand on. Neither of them moved.

From the inside of her coat she took the tessar-na — the bone token, no bigger than a child's tooth — and she held it for a moment in her closed fist as if she were going to put it back. She did not put it back.

She set the bone token on the wet sand between them. It would, she knew, sink before either of them could decide what it meant—
